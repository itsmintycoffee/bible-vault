## Key Findings by Book

### EXODUS
**Overall Composition:**
- **Yahwist (J):** 25-30% - Core narrative framework (deliverance story)
- **Elohist (E):** 15-20% - Alternative narratives, covenant code
- **Priestly (P):** 45-50% - Increasingly dominant toward end (tabernacle)

**Notable Features:**
- Chapters 1-11: Narrative-heavy (J/E)
- Chapters 12-18: Mixed (Passover and wilderness stories)
- Chapters 19-24: Legal frameworks (E's Covenant Code)
- Chapters 25-40: **Priestly dominated** (tabernacle descriptions)

**Key Sources:**
- J: Exodus/deliverance narrative, divine encounters
- E: Alternative plague accounts, covenant emphasis
- P: Tabernacle instructions, genealogies, chronology

---

### LEVITICUS
**Overall Composition:**
- **Yahwist (J):** ~0% - Virtually absent
- **Elohist (E):** ~0% - Virtually absent
- **Priestly (P):** ~90%+ - Dominant throughout
- **Deuteronomist (D):** ~0% - Absent

**Notable Features:**
- **Unique among Torah books** - almost entirely one source
- Represents P's legal and ritual system comprehensively
- No competing alternative accounts from J/E
- The Holiness Code (H, chapters 17-26) is P's ethical framework

**Key Content:**
- Chapters 1-7: Offerings (burnt, grain, peace, sin, guilt)
- Chapters 8-10: Priestly ordination and duties
- Chapters 11-16: Purity laws (animals, disease, discharges, Atonement)
- Chapters 17-26: Holiness Code (ethics, festivals, jubilee, vows)
- Chapter 27: Votive offerings

---

### NUMBERS
**Overall Composition:**
- **Yahwist (J):** 10-15% - Wilderness narratives
- **Elohist (E):** 10-15% - Alternative narratives, ethics
- **Priestly (P):** 60-70% - Census, law, ritual
- **Deuteronomist (D):** ~0% - Minimal

**Notable Features:**
- **Most complex interweaving** of sources
- P's census lists and organizational structure frame the book
- J/E narratives (murmuring, rebellion, spy story) inserted within P framework
- Shows how different sources were woven together chronologically

**Key Divisions:**
- Chapters 1-10: **P dominant** (census, organization, rituals)
- Chapters 11-14: **Narrative-heavy** (murmuring, spies - J/E)
- Chapters 15-19: **P dominant** (laws and rituals)
- Chapters 20-21: **Mixed** (wilderness journey stories)
- Chapters 22-25: **J dominant** (Balaam narrative)
- Chapters 26-36: **P dominant** (second census, laws, land distribution)

---

### DEUTERONOMY
**Overall Composition:**
- **Yahwist (J):** 1-5% - Minimal (Blessing of Moses)
- **Elohist (E):** 5-10% - Closing chapters
- **Priestly (P):** 5-10% - Closing notices and appendix
- **Deuteronomist (D):** 80%+ - The book itself

**Notable Features:**
- **Unique voice and consistency** throughout
- Represents a coherent theological vision
- The Deuteronomist likely combines two versions (Dtr1, Dtr2)
- Scholars see D as product of 7th century BCE court/prophetic reform

**Key Characteristics:**
- Rhetorical, sermonic tone
- Emphasis on centralized cult at one place
- Covenantal love language
- Historical reviews and exhortations
- Extensive blessing/curse material

## Scholarly Attribution Methods

The data presented here is based on scholarly analysis that examines:

1. **Vocabulary and style** - Word choices, phrases, theological terminology
2. **Divine names** - Use of YHWH (J) vs. Elohim (E)
3. **Doublets** - Parallel accounts of same event (e.g., plagues)
4. **Anachronisms** - References to institutions/customs from different periods
5. **Theological themes** - J's anthropomorphic God vs. P's transcendent approach
6. **Legal codes** - Different legal traditions (E's Covenant Code vs. P's elaborate system)
7. **Narrative continuity** - How stories flow and connect

## Scholarly Consensus and Variation

### Points of High Consensus
✅ Leviticus is almost entirely P  
✅ Deuteronomy is predominantly D  
✅ Exodus contains substantial J and E narratives  
✅ Numbers combines P organizational material with J/E narratives  

### Areas of Scholarly Debate
⚠️ Exact boundaries between J and E passages  
⚠️ Whether a substantial E source really exists (some scholars argue it's fragmentary)  
⚠️ Dating of sources (traditional: J=10th c., E=9th c., D=7th c., P=6th-5th c.)  
⚠️ Nature of redaction process (was it gradual or orchestrated?)  

### Modern Trends
Recent scholarship increasingly:
- Questions the classical JEDP framework
- Emphasizes redactional work (R)
- Proposes "fragmentary" rather than "documentary" models
- Dates final form to Persian period (5th-4th c. BCE)
- Acknowledges literary sophistication and intentional composition
